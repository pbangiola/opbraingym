The Brain Gym curriculum tree

Learn a skill, use it to make something, and explain the choices you made. Shared foundations support every branch. Each project adds technical skills and practice in planning, working safely, and finishing well.

Working curriculum · Proposed pathways and assessments, not a current class schedule. This is a Brain Gym adaptation informed by NCCER Core and OSHA training subjects; it does not award NCCER credentials, OSHA cards or professional licenses.

The branches

These are areas to explore, with examples of what learners could make and demonstrate. The links back to the roots show how the same foundations appear in different work.

Carpenter

Lay out a part, choose a joint, and check fit and finish.

Project progression
Cutting board or small carving → simple box → frame or coat rack.
Evidence
Measured drawing, joint sample, finished object and care notes.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Power tools, Drawings & design, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Woodworking pilot. Machine work stays within individual instruction and authorization.

Electrician

Read a simple circuit and explain the path of current.

Project progression
Battery-powered light or continuity model.
Evidence
Circuit drawing, working low-voltage model and explanation of a fault.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Drawings & design, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Start with isolated, current-limited low-voltage teaching equipment; no mains installation authorization.

Welder

Compare joint types and explain heat, fumes and spark hazards.

Project progression
Cold mock-up of a metal bracket → supervised demonstration in a suitable facility.
Evidence
Joint drawing, assembly mock-up and hazard plan.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Power tools, Drawings & design, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Future specialist pathway; no live welding in the home-shop pilot.

Mason

Use level, plumb and a bond pattern to explain stability.

Project progression
Small dry-laid model or tile layout.
Evidence
Layout drawing, model and explanation of load and movement.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Drawings & design, Rigging & load awareness, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Start with small dry models; dust-producing work and structural construction need separate controls.

Plumber

Choose a fitting and explain flow, slope or an accessible joint.

Project progression
Unpressurized water-flow board or removable pipe model.
Evidence
System sketch, parts list and observed flow test.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Drawings & design, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Models first; no gas piping, live building connections or licensed-work sign-off.

Designer / drawer

Translate a need into dimensions, a model and a test.

Project progression
Measured sketch → CAD model → printed jig or prototype.
Evidence
Revision history, fit check and explanation of a changed dimension.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Power tools, Drawings & design, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Connects every branch to design and the existing 3D-printing references.

Leader / project manager

Lead a briefing, assign roles, verify controls, and review the result.

Project progression
Manage a two-person build, then a project with several handoffs.
Evidence
Project brief, task / hazard plan, readiness check and closeout review.
Shared roots
Orientation & purpose, Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Power tools, Drawings & design, Rigging & load awareness, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Practiced in every project; OSHA-informed management competencies sit here and in the trunk.

Driver

Plan a route and explain balance, visibility, restraint and stopping.

Project progression
Move light stock on a suitable hand cart through a marked route.
Evidence
Route sketch, load check and observed controlled movement.
Shared roots
Safety, Measurement & math, Hand tools, Drawings & design, Rigging & load awareness, Communication, Working with others, Materials handling
Teaching scope
Material-movement learning first; powered equipment, road driving and licensing are separate pathways.

Project management is a practical skill

A good plan accounts for the people, tools, materials and hazards as well as the finished object. The workflow below is Brain Gym’s proposed application of OSHA’s safety-management and job-hazard-analysis principles. [6] [7] [8]

StageProject-management actionSafety-management actionEvidenceOSHA principle
1 · DefineDescribe the user, purpose, dimensions, quality target, time and materials budget.Agree on who leads and who can call a stop. Everyone can report a concern.A short project brief and acceptance criteria.Management leadership; participation.
2 · Break down the workList tasks, dependencies, estimated time and role handoffs.For each task, record the hazard, who is exposed and the planned control. Include setup, cleanup and maintenance.Task sequence + job hazard analysis.Hazard identification and assessment.
3 · Choose the methodSelect materials, tools, joining method and the order of operations.First remove or substitute the hazardous operation where possible; then use engineering controls, work rules and appropriate PPE.Method / material list and named owners for controls.Hazard prevention and control.
4 · Check readinessConfirm stock, drawings, time, workspace and the next handoff.Verify instruction, tool authorization, guards, work holding, dust / chemical controls, access and emergency arrangements. Unresolved control: do not start.Instructor-reviewed go / no-go check.Education, training and coordination.
5 · Make and monitorCheck dimensions and fit at planned points; record changes.Stop and review when the material, tool, person or method changes. Report a near miss; do not improvise around a failed control.Progress / change notes and inspection checks.Participation; ongoing hazard review.
6 · Close and improveTest against the brief, compare estimates with results, and hand over care instructions.Leave tools and space ready for the next user. Review incidents and near misses for contributing conditions and corrective actions.Finished object + closeout record with improvement owner and follow-up.Program evaluation; incident investigation.

For a young child working with a parent, the plan can be a sketch and a spoken briefing. The instructor assigns age-appropriate steps; the adult’s presence does not authorize a child to use a machine. More experienced learners produce and lead a fuller plan.

One project record

  • Brief: what we are making, for whom, and what counts as finished.
  • Plan: drawing, parts, cost, tasks, time and who does each step.
  • Hazard analysis: task → hazard / exposed people → control → responsible person → readiness check.
  • Checkpoints: instructor review before unfamiliar operations; inspection before the next dependent step.
  • Closeout: result, care instructions, cleanup, near misses and one change for next time.

OSHA learning pathways and the competencies they inform

There are two connected tracks: learners practice the habits during projects; adults responsible for instruction and shop operation can pursue formal training with the appropriate provider. The proposed order starts with General Industry hazard awareness and branches into standards, management and specialist study. It is not a single credential ladder.

Learning pathwaySubject focusBrain Gym application — proposedAdult development route / source
General Industry Outreach · 10 / 30 hoursHazard awareness; 30-hour training suits people with safety responsibilities.Learners recognize and report hazards; project leaders practice a pre-task briefing.Adult foundation, with 30-hour as the proposed lead-instructor starting point. [2] [3]
OSHA #511General Industry standards, policies and procedures.Find the applicable guidance and turn it into a project-specific requirement.Adult standards study; 30-hour Outreach is not equivalent to #511. [4]
OSHA #7500 / #2455Safety and health management systems / programs.Assign responsibility, plan controls, train people and review performance.Adult management study; apply principles throughout project planning. [5]
OSHA #7515Job hazard analysis.Break a job into steps; identify hazards and controls before starting.Targeted planning study; informs the task / hazard record. [5] [8]
OSHA #7505Incident investigation and analysis.Explain contributing conditions, choose corrective actions and check that they worked.Adult investigation study; use age-appropriate near-miss reviews in projects. [5]
OSHA #2045 / #7115Machine safeguarding / hazardous-energy control.Identify machine hazards and when work must stop for an authorized person.Specialist instructor learning; servicing and energy isolation need their own procedures and training. [5]
OSHA #521 / #2255 / #3095Industrial hygiene / ergonomics / electrical hazards.Include exposures, posture, handling and electrical conditions in the plan.Select adult specialties to match actual equipment and activities. [5]
Safety & Health Fundamentals · General IndustryA formal OTI Education Center certificate program.Broader adult competence to develop and evaluate the shop’s safety system.At least seven approved courses and 68 contact hours; confirm the current required / elective combination with the center. [9] [10]
OSHA #501 · optional trainer routeTraining to deliver General Industry Outreach.Plan and teach formal Outreach only after meeting authorization requirements.Requires #511 and qualifying General Industry safety experience; ordinarily five years, with specified substitutions. Not an automatic next step after OSHA 30. [3] [11]

An Outreach card records course completion; it is not a professional license or certification. Brain Gym’s own demonstrations and tool sign-offs are separate from OSHA training and NCCER credentials. No completed training or authorization is claimed here. [3]

Use the reference pages during the work

  • Fasteners — choose a connection and explain how it engages the material.
  • Getting sticky stuff off — plan cleanup and surface compatibility before applying adhesive or finish.
  • 3D printing setup and build plates — justify the printing method and record the chosen profile.

Evidence of learning

Use the same progression in each branch. Record the demonstrated skill, conditions, support needed and date. A finished object alone does not show safe technique or good judgment.

StageWhat the learner demonstratesEvidence
ExplainNames the tool / method, describes its purpose and recognizes when to stop.Conversation, sketch or labeled example.
Practice with guidanceCompletes an assigned operation with appropriate prompts and supervision.Instructor observation and a sample.
Apply within an approved taskChooses and carries out a method within the instructor’s defined boundaries.Observed setup, work and checks; the amount of support is recorded.
Plan, explain and improveCoordinates a suitable task, explains tradeoffs and revises the next attempt.Briefing, project record, product and review.

Progress is specific to the task and equipment. It does not automatically transfer to a different machine, material, workplace or legal scope of practice. The curriculum grows by adding demonstrated skills and increasingly complex projects.
